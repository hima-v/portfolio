  • The USDA wanted to know how much US corn is chopped for silage (cattle feed) versus harvested for grain, without waiting months for survey numbers. Our team of five built a system that makes that call field by field from free Sentinel-2 and Landsat images, then adds it up to county and state estimates. We built it in 22.5 hours at Aggie Square in Sacramento.

    I worked on the Bayesian modeling that estimates fields hidden by clouds, the retrieval step that checks each county against USDA reports and cites them, and making the NVIDIA Nemotron agents reliable.

    • Grand Prize, judged by USDA, NVIDIA, Niantic Spatial and UC Davis
    • 42 of 44 labelled US fields called right (95.5%), with no US labels in training
    • 4 of 5 graded states within 5 points of USDA's 2025 numbers
    • 13.3 to 1.4 average point gap in TX, CA and CO after adding USDA history

    Python, scikit-learn, GeoPandas, rasterio, Sentinel-2, Landsat 8/9, FastAPI, Leaflet, NVIDIA Nemotron, pytest

  • My friends kept losing tracks they loved because Discover Weekly refreshes every Monday. This app copies new tracks into a private playlist each week. I built it as a small distributed system instead of one script: the API handles sign-in, a worker does the sync, and the database keeps a history of every run.

VIA Lab, Sep 2026 – now

What makes a short video feel intense?

With Prof. Dongyu Liu, I'm studying how viewers react to short videos and which moments, sounds and visuals drive that reaction. I started by reproducing two published studies end to end, one using multimodal models as stand-in study participants and one using a random forest baseline. Now I'm testing models that look at how features change over time inside a video.

Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn, Gemini, Qwen

GitHub

Advanced NLP, Fall 2026

Can an AI improve how it improves?

With Ketki Kulkarni and Shawn Bowser, I presented Meta's HyperAgents paper. It merges the agent that solves a task and the agent that edits it into one program, so the system can rewrite how it improves, not just its answers. We covered results in coding, paper review, robotics and math grading, then pushed on the gaps: it matches past reviewer decisions rather than judging quality, isn't clearly better than a hand-tuned baseline, and costs about 83.6M tokens per run.

Our slides (PDF) The paper

Advanced NLP project, Sep 2026 – now

streaMRAG: answering questions about live video

A retrieval setup for video streams that needs no training. It checks whether each retrieved moment actually supports an answer, reasons about when things happened, and keeps a compact memory within a fixed token budget so answers stay grounded in what was seen.

Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face, vision-language models, NLI, RAG
